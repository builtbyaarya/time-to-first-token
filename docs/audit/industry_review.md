# Industry review: Time to First Token

Reviewer role: data center development director (site selection, interconnection, EPC). Scope: README, methodology, js/model.js, data/pack.json, index.html, js/app.js, and the 300 MW Northern Virginia hybrid sample study. Items are ordered most severe first. Line numbers refer to the files as read on 2026-10-24.

1. **Blocker.** Where: `js/model.js` L145-147 and L213-214; study §1 ("first token July 2031 ... full grid power May 2032"), §3 ("sized at 262 MW: 150 MW of IT"), Table 1 (2,500 racks). What is wrong: in a hybrid plan the IST and the full rack install are gated only on the onsite plant, so the study serves the first token from all 300 MW of racks on a plant sized for 150 MW of IT, ten months before the grid arrives. What it should be: for hybrid, first token comes from the onsite share of racks (`phaseFactor = hybrid_onsite_share` unless the phase lever is on), the remaining racks are gated on utility energization, and the study says so in §1 and §3. Why: a reader who knows the plant is 262 MW and the hall is 300 MW IT at PUE 1.25 (375 MW facility load) will stop reading at §1.

2. **Blocker.** Where: `js/model.js` L238-245 (`elecM` on full `mwhYear`) and L249-251 (fuel on `share`); study Table 4 (Electricity 229, Natural gas 34). What is wrong: hybrid opex charges the full year's electricity at the grid price and then adds gas for the half the plant serves, so the onsite half is paid for twice. What it should be: electricity = E x (1 - share) x price, plus fuel for the share; for the VA sample roughly $115M of electricity, not $229M. Why: the $474M-684M opex headline in §1 is overstated by about $115M a year.

3. **Blocker.** Where: `js/model.js` L245 (`elecM * (bridge ? 0 : 1)`), `js/app.js` L156 ("replaced by fuel"); study Table 5 ("Power: bridge ... opex 384"). What is wrong: a bridge plan is shown with no electricity cost for ever; bridge means gas until energization, then grid. What it should be: either a steady-state opex with grid electricity (and the plant as backup) or two figures, year one on gas and steady state on grid, labelled. Also the gas price is Henry Hub (`gas_price_usd_per_mmbtu`), not a delivered price to a Virginia site; label it as a hub price and flag the basis and transport adder as an assumption. Why: the sensitivity table makes bridge look $195M a year cheaper than hybrid for a reason that is a modelling artefact.

4. **Blocker.** Where: `js/model.js` L140 (`electrical` pred includes `"genset"` and `"air"`); study §1 critical path "Survey, Design and engineering, Standby generators, Electrical fit-out ...". What is wrong: the 18-month standby generator lead gates the *start* of the whole electrical fit-out, which is what puts gensets on the sample's critical path. Gensets sit on exterior pads and are set late in the fit-out; switchgear, UPS and busway go in without them. Likewise the air permit restricts installation of the emission units, not the switchgear rooms (PSD: no "begin actual construction" of the major source before the permit, 40 CFR 52.21(a)(2)(iii), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-52/subpart-A/section-52.21). What it should be: electrical pred = [(shell, 0.67), switchgear]; gensets and the air permit gate Cx L3-L4 (or the tail of the fit-out via a fraction), and the onsite plant install as now. Why: the headline critical path of the sample is an artefact of this gate.

5. **Blocker.** Where: `js/model.js` L128-131 (onsite plant tasks); `docs/methodology.md` §2 table; study §3. What is wrong: a 262 MW gas turbine plant has no gas supply in the plan: no pipeline lateral, no transport or supply agreement, no metering/compression, no pipeline right-of-way or state siting step. What it should be: a `gas_supply_months` task (lateral and supply agreement) gating `geninst`, labelled assumption if no public figure, and a sentence in §3 naming it. Why: it is the first question any investor asks about bridge power, and the schedule claims the plant is critical-path-ready at month 36 with nothing feeding it.

6. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` L116 (`design` after `survey`), L135-138 (long-lead orders after `design`); study Table 2 (long-lead orders released at month 10.5). What is wrong: design is serial after a 4.5-month survey, and every long-lead order waits for the full 6-month design package, so orders go out at month 10.5. In 2026 practice the basis of design releases switchgear, gensets and transformer reservations, and geotech runs in parallel with schematic design. What it should be: `design` pred [(survey, 0.5)]; long-lead pred [(design, 0.5)] with the pack note already in `design_months` ("early design packages release ... the long-lead orders"). Why: the pack's own note says this; the model does not do it, and it is why the sample's critical path runs through survey and design.

7. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` L144-146 (only `cx5` waits for power). What is wrong: in a grid-only plan Cx L3-L4 (start-up and functional tests of switchgear, UPS, chillers) runs with no permanent power and then the site idles until energization. Functional testing needs utility power or at least the gensets. What it should be: gate `cx34` on power as well (or on gensets for a grid plan, with the IST on utility). Why: the "Power: grid, 76 months" row and any grid plan's Gantt show L3-L4 finishing 20 months before the power that tests it exists.

8. **Should fix.** Where: `index.html` L71-73 (Redundancy: N+1 / 2N / Tier IV); `docs/methodology.md` §1 and §5; `js/model.js` L272 (lever "2N redundancy: Concurrently maintainable power paths"); study Table 5 ("Redundancy: tier4"). What is wrong: Tier IV is an Uptime Institute certification of the whole facility (fault tolerant), not a redundancy topology; 2N is one way to meet it. "Concurrently maintainable" is the Tier III criterion, not what 2N buys you; 2N buys fault tolerance. What it should be: a redundancy choice (N+1 / 2N / 2N+1) and a separate "Uptime Tier certification" option that adds the TCDD and TCCF time and the premium; lever text "2N: a second full power path, fault tolerant". Source: https://www.digitaledgedc.com/?p=7348 (Tier III concurrently maintainable, Tier IV fault tolerant, Uptime is the only certifying body).

9. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` L122-123 and L264 ("Order transformers at project start ... at risk of a spec change"); study §3 ("The transformers are ordered when the study ends"). What is wrong: for a utility-served load the utility (Dominion, AEP, Georgia Power) procures the LPTs for its substation; the developer can only pre-order its own customer-side units or fund the utility's reservation. The lever does not say who orders what. What it should be: "Reserve transformer slots at project start (customer-side units, or a funded utility reservation)" and a line in §3 saying which side of the fence the 29-month lead applies to. Why: an interconnection person will ask whether the developer can even place this order.

10. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` L121 (`Large-load interconnection study, ${iso}`); `data/pack.json` `large_load_study_months` note; study §2 and Table 2 ("Large-load interconnection study, PJM"). What is wrong: PJM runs no large-load queue; the PJM Board said it will not create one, and the load interconnection study for a Northern Virginia customer is Dominion's own process (https://www.zeroemissiongrid.com/zeg-blog/pjm-large-load-interconnection-process-2026/). The pack note also leans on "FERC Order 2023 sets a 150-day cluster study for generators", which is a generator rule with no bearing on loads, and calls the study an "EMT study", which is one study type inside a load process. What it should be: label the task "Utility load interconnection study (Dominion)" where the ISO is PJM, keep the ISO label where the ISO really runs it (ERCOT batch, MISO ERAS, SPP HILL), and drop the Order 2023 clause.

11. **Should fix.** Where: study Table 1 row "Standby generation 1.75[16] kW per kW IT"; `data/pack.json` `onsite_oversize_factor` note. What is wrong: 1.75 is the onsite plant sizing factor, not standby generation, and it is derived from a standby genset sizing guide (Generator Source) then applied to a prime-power turbine plant. What it should be: label the row "Onsite plant sizing" and state the plant's N+1 unit arrangement and net site rating (MW at site conditions) separately; say standby gensets are sized per the redundancy choice.

12. **Should fix.** Where: study Table 5 ("Cooling: air, 54 months, capex 14,000"); `scripts/make_report.py` sensitivity rows. What is wrong: the study runs air cooling as a valid, cheaper, faster case for GB200 NVL72 at 120 kW per rack, while the page itself warns air is impractical above 50 kW per rack (`js/model.js` L189). What it should be: suppress the row above `air_cooling_max_kw_per_rack` or print it with the warning.

13. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` L304-307 (risk impact on first token, sort by likelihood x impact); study Table 6 (Transformer slip 0, Interconnection study delay 0, Utility energization delay 0). What is wrong: in a hybrid plan the three grid risks score zero and sink to the bottom; for a Northern Virginia hybrid, a utility delay delays half the capacity and the biggest number in the study (May 2032 full power). What it should be: report impact on first token and on full power, and rank on the larger. Why: a NoVA reader who sees "utility energization delay: impact 0" will distrust the register.

14. **Should fix.** Where: `js/app.js` L184 (`s.id === "labour" ? "%" : "mo"`). What is wrong: the labour stress chip reads "+3 %" while the model adds 3 months to the electrical fit-out (`js/model.js` L108). What it should be: "+3 mo".

15. **Should fix.** Where: `js/app.js` L88 (`#in-target-hint` set to the GPU rack text). What is wrong: the hint under "Target go-live" reads "72 GPUs per rack, liquid cooled, 120 to 132 kW". What it should be: that text belongs under the GPU generation / rack density fields; the target hint should say what "versus target" is measured against.

16. **Should fix.** Where: `js/app.js` L85 (grid hint: "Wait for the utility; nothing to permit but the standby generators."). What is wrong: zoning, site plan, building permit and, with towers, the water approvals are all still on a grid-only plan. What it should be: "Wait for the utility; the only air permit is the minor-source one for the standby generators."

17. **Should fix.** Where: `js/model.js` task list (no accelerator order task), L147 (`compute` after `cx5`); `data/pack.json` `stress_gpu_months` (assumption). What is wrong: $7.5B to $11.1B of NVL72 racks have no order or lead-time bar; the only GPU item is a 3-month stress. "GPU racks installed" at month 49.7 implies the racks are on the shelf. What it should be: an "Accelerator order and allocation" lead task released from design (or project start), with a labelled lead-time assumption, so the stress has something to slip.

18. **Should fix.** Where: `docs/methodology.md` §5 ("metro factor: `capex_metro_index__<state>`"); study §7 ("f_metro = 1.00", "scaled by the ... metro factors"). What is wrong: the pack has no `capex_metro_index` ids (zero matches in `data/pack.json`), so the metro factor is always 1.00 and the study claims a scaling that never happens. What it should be: add the index (Cushman & Wakefield or Turner & Townsend publish city indices) or remove the claim from the methodology and the study text.

19. **Should fix.** Where: study §7 ("Staff, maintenance and other opex is 0 to 1[46] $M per MW-year"; "3.16 $ per MMBtu"; "11.3[44] $M per MW"; "bring-up 1 months"). What is wrong: a 0.7-1.4 range printed as "0 to 1" by integer rounding, and currency written after the number. What it should be: "$0.7M to $1.4M per MW-year", "$3.16 per MMBtu", "$11.3M per MW", "bring-up 1 month"; round per-MW and per-unit figures to one decimal.

20. **Should fix.** Where: study Table 4 (Electricity "229 / 229"), `js/app.js` L157 (gas tagged "range" with one value), L161 ("IT utilization 0.7 assumption"), L148 (KPI "Capex, facility" shows `costs.site`, which includes the onsite plant). What is wrong: the pack carries ranges for utilization (0.6-0.8) and PUE (1.15-1.35) but electricity is printed as a single value in a low/high table; gas has no range yet wears the tag; utilization is tagged assumption in the UI and sourced in the pack; the KPI label does not match the table's "Facility and power". What it should be: propagate the ranges or drop the tags; make the labels agree with the pack kinds and with each other.

21. **Nit.** Where: `js/model.js` L120 ("Cooling-water discharge permit"), `js/app.js` L87 ("Needs a water permit"), `data/pack.json` `water_permit_months` (Washington individual NPDES as US default). What is wrong: tower sites are usually gated by water supply (a utility allocation or withdrawal permit) and blowdown often goes to sanitary sewer under a pretreatment permit, not an individual NPDES. What it should be: "Water supply agreement and discharge approval", with the 8.1-month figure labelled as a proxy.

22. **Nit.** Where: `js/model.js` L143 (Cx L1-L2 at 60% of fit-out), L13 (STAGES: "Rows, containment, empty racks"). What is wrong: L1 is factory witness testing during the lead time and L2 is receipt inspection, so the task is in the wrong place (harmless, it has float); and NVL72 arrives as an integrated rack, so "empty racks" in the white space stage does not fit the accelerator the tool defaults to. What it should be: start L1-L2 with the long-lead orders; word the white space stage as "rows, containment, power and liquid drops".

23. **Nit.** Where: study Figure 1 and Table 2 ("Utility energization (wait for the utility) ... 29.1 months"); `js/app.js` Gantt duration labels. What is wrong: the wait is drawn as a 29-month bar, which reads as "energization takes 29 months". What it should be: draw it as a milestone with a not-before date ("not before month 66") or label the bar "wait".

24. **Nit.** Where: `data/pack.json` `capex_recip_per_kw` (2019$, unescalated) against `capex_gas_turbine_per_kw` (2023$) and the C&W 2026 facility costs; `lead_gas_turbine_months` (frame-class Siemens/GE backlog) against `capex_gas_turbine_per_kw` (aeroderivative); `pue__liquid_tower` (AWS fleet, mostly air-cooled evaporative) used as "liquid with towers". What is wrong: mixed dollar years and mixed equipment classes inside one line. What it should be: escalate to one dollar year and say so in each note; pick one turbine class for both lead time and cost; label the tower PUE as a fleet proxy.

25. **Nit.** Where: study §2 ("only 13[10] %", "9[34] kW"), §5 (one sentence, citations out of order [33] [31] [34]), §7 ("Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, puts the US fleet at a PUE of 1.40" inside the water paragraph), appendix 6 ("Grid region, Virginia = 0 [sourced]"). What is wrong: citation markers inside numbers, a stray comma, an orphan PUE sentence in the water paragraph, and text-valued pack entries printed as "= 0". What it should be: markers after the unit ("13% [10]"), the fleet PUE moved to the PUE sentence, and text values printed as their text in the appendix.
